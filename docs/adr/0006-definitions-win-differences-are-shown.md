# A term's definition wins over a filed figure, and every difference is shown

When a company files a figure under a term's tag that breaks the term's own definition, the tool shows the figure the definition gives, marked as a calculated number. The filed figure stays attached to it, marked as a doubtful number with the check it failed. Costco's fiscal 2019 gross profit is the first case: it filed 16,465 million, its gross margin on net sales only, where revenue minus cost of revenue gives 19,817 million.

Operating income is taken as filed, because it is the audited line. Whatever lies between it and research and development plus selling, general and administrative is shown as other operating expenses, a calculated number, so every fiscal year adds up from revenue to operating income. Operating income is checked against the total operating expenses, or the total costs and expenses, the company reports, where it reports one.

Every fiscal year that arrives in an audited annual report is shown, including the earlier years the first machine-readable reports carried, back to about fiscal 2007.

We chose this because it is what an investment adviser does with filed statements: keep years comparable by holding each term to one definition, trust the audited totals, and footnote every adjustment rather than hide it. It keeps the settled rule that nothing unsure is hidden.

## Consequences

- The money flow has eight terms, with other operating expenses between selling, general and administrative and operating income.
- A doubtful number can appear as the figure itself, or as a filed figure set aside behind a calculated number.
- Companies that report no total, such as Costco, have operating income that cannot be checked independently. Their other operating expenses, such as Costco's preopening costs, still show what lies between the lines.
